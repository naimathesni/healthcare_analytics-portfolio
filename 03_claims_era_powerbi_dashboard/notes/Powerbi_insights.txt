Healthcare Claims, ERA & Denial Analytics Dashboard - Power BI Insights

Project Overview

This Power BI dashboard analyzes synthetic healthcare revenue-cycle data across claims, ERA records, denials, payers, providers, and enrollment readiness.

The purpose of the dashboard is to help revenue-cycle, EDI, and operations teams monitor claim outcomes, rejection risk, denial patterns, ERA/payment lag, payer performance, provider/practice performance, and enrollment-related claim risk.

Executive Summary Insights

1. Rejected claims are the largest claim issue category, with 525 rejected claims and a 35.00% rejection rate.

2. Enrollment/Configuration is the top denial category, indicating payer setup, NPI/PTAN, claims enrollment, ERA readiness, and clearinghouse configuration risk.

3. 1,050 claims do not have ERA records. Many are submitted, pending, or rejected, but payer-level ERA coverage should still be monitored.

4. 200 ERA records are over 21 days, showing potential remittance turnaround risk.

5. 900 claims were submitted while not claims-ready, supporting claims-readiness validation before submission.

Payer Performance Insights

1. Payer performance should not be evaluated only by total claim count.

2. Rejection rate, denial rate, average ERA lag, claims without ERA, and total paid amount give a more complete payer performance view.

3. Some payers show higher rejection rates even when total claim volume is similar.

4. Payers with high ERA lag may create payment posting delays and additional follow-up work.

5. Payer-level analysis helps operations prioritize which payer workflows should be reviewed first.

Denial Analysis Insights

1. Enrollment/Configuration is the largest denial category.

2. Most denials are marked preventable, which suggests opportunity for front-end validation and operational controls.

3. Denied amount should be reviewed along with denial count because high-volume categories are not always the highest financial-impact categories.

4. Payer-level denial patterns can help prioritize follow-up with payers that generate high denial volume or preventable denials.

ERA & Payment Analysis Insights

1. 450 claims have ERA records, while 1,050 claims do not have ERA records.

2. Claims without ERA are not always errors. Submitted, pending, and rejected claims may not have ERA yet.

3. Average ERA lag is 20.16 days, with 200 ERA records taking more than 21 days.

4. Payers with high average ERA lag may create payment posting delays and additional follow-up work.

5. ERA readiness should be monitored separately from claims readiness because successful claim submission does not always mean remittance flow is complete.

Enrollment Impact Insights

1. 1,200 claims are tied to action-required enrollment records, showing significant operational risk.

2. 900 claims were submitted while not claims-ready, which can create front-end rejection and denial risk.

3. 187 claims are tied to NPI mismatch, highlighting the importance of credentialed NPI vs enrolled NPI validation.

4. 1,200 claims are tied to clearinghouse gaps, suggesting clearinghouse portal and configuration readiness should be validated before go-live.

5. Claims readiness risk is more urgent than ERA readiness risk because claims must submit successfully before ERA can be received.

Provider / Practice Drilldown Insights

1. Practices with high rejected claim counts should be reviewed for claims submission readiness, payer setup, and clearinghouse configuration issues.

2. Specialty-level denial patterns can help identify workflow or documentation areas that may need closer review.

3. Paid amount by practice helps distinguish high-volume practices from high-financial-impact practices.

4. Provider/practice drilldowns help operations prioritize targeted follow-up instead of treating all claim issues the same.

Recommendations

1. Add claims-readiness validation before claim submission.

2. Monitor rejected claims separately from denied claims because rejected claims represent an earlier revenue-cycle failure.

3. Prioritize Enrollment/Configuration denials because they are the largest denial category.

4. Validate credentialed NPI against enrolled NPI before payer submission.

5. Create payer-specific enrollment and clearinghouse setup checklists.

6. Monitor payer-level rejection rate, denial rate, ERA lag, and claims without ERA together.

7. Track claims readiness and ERA readiness separately.

8. Build an operational priority queue using rejected claims, denied claims, issue billed amount, preventable denials, and enrollment/configuration denial counts.

Analyst Takeaway

The main takeaway from this dashboard is that claims, ERA, denials, and enrollment readiness should not be analyzed in isolation.

A useful healthcare revenue-cycle dashboard should connect claim outcomes to payer, provider, enrollment readiness, denial category, ERA status, and payment lag.

This dashboard shows that payer setup, claims enrollment, ERA readiness, NPI/PTAN validation, and clearinghouse configuration can affect claim submission, denial risk, remittance flow, and payment timing.
