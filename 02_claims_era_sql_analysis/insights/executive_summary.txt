Healthcare Claims & ERA SQL Analysis - Executive Summary

Project Overview

This project uses MySQL to analyze synthetic healthcare revenue-cycle data across claims, ERA records, denials, payer information, provider information, and enrollment readiness.

The purpose of the project is to identify operational issues that may affect claim submission, payer adjudication, ERA receipt, payment timing, and denial management.

Business Problem

Healthcare organizations need visibility into claim outcomes, payer performance, denial root causes, ERA/payment lag, and enrollment-related risks.

Claims and ERA workflows can be disrupted when:
- Claims are submitted before enrollment is ready
- Provider NPI or PTAN information does not match payer setup
- Clearinghouse configuration is incomplete
- Payer ID or routing is incorrect
- ERA readiness is incomplete
- Payer-specific enrollment requirements are missed
- Denials are preventable but not monitored effectively

This SQL analysis helps identify where those risks appear in claims, denial, ERA, payer, provider, and enrollment data.

Key Findings

1. Rejected claims are a major revenue-cycle risk.

The rejection rate is 35.00%, with 525 rejected claims out of 1,500 total claims.

Rejected claims represent an early failure in the revenue cycle because they may not reach full payer adjudication. This can delay payment and ERA flow before denial management begins.

2. Enrollment/Configuration is the largest denial category.

Enrollment/Configuration denials account for 75 of 188 total denials.

This category is directly connected to payer setup, NPI/PTAN validation, claims enrollment, ERA enrollment, and clearinghouse configuration.

3. Most denials are preventable.

151 of 188 denials are marked preventable.

This suggests that improved front-end controls, payer-specific checklists, and enrollment validation could reduce avoidable denial volume.

4. ERA/payment lag should be monitored by payer.

The average ERA lag is 20.16 days, and 200 ERA records are over 21 days.

Delayed ERA receipt can create payment posting delays, follow-up work, and reduced visibility into claim resolution.

5. Enrollment readiness is connected to claims risk.

1,200 claims are tied to action-required enrollment records, and 900 claims were submitted while the enrollment record was not claims-ready.

This supports the need for pre-submission claims readiness validation.

6. NPI mismatch and clearinghouse gaps are operational risks.

Claims tied to NPI mismatch and clearinghouse gaps indicate that provider identifiers and clearinghouse setup should be validated before claim submission and ERA expectations.

Recommendations

1. Add a claims-readiness validation checkpoint before claim submission.

Validate credentialing status, claims enrollment status, NPI match status, payer setup, clearinghouse configuration, and blocker reason before claims are sent.

2. Monitor rejected claims separately from denied claims.

Rejected claims should be treated as a front-end claims submission issue, while denied claims should be analyzed after payer adjudication.

3. Prioritize Enrollment/Configuration denials.

This denial category is strongly tied to operational setup and may be reduced through payer enrollment and clearinghouse readiness controls.

4. Create a payer-level performance report.

Track payer rejection rate, denial rate, ERA lag, claims without ERA, preventable denials, and enrollment readiness issues together.

5. Build an enrollment blocker priority queue.

Rank blocker reasons by rejected claims, denied claims, issue billed amount, and enrollment/configuration denial count.

6. Track claims readiness and ERA readiness separately.

A provider/payer record may be ready for claims but not ready for ERA, or vice versa. These should be monitored as separate operational controls.

Limitations

- The dataset is synthetic and does not represent actual payer, provider, patient, employer, or production data.
- The analysis focuses on claims, ERA, denials, and enrollment readiness, not full AR follow-up or payment posting.
- The synthetic dataset intentionally includes operational issues for analysis practice.
- Financial results are for portfolio demonstration only.
- The payer performance score is a custom prioritization model and would need stakeholder validation in a real business environment.
